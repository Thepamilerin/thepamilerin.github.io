title: "Did the plastic bag charge actually work?"
role: "Independent analysis"
year: 2025
duration: "Defra data, 2016-17 to 2024-25"
outcomeSummary: "The charge cut bags per person by 81%, but the doubling to 10p added almost nothing, and the public data cannot say whether people switched to another bag"
overview: "A statistical analysis of nine years of Defra data on single-use plastic carrier bags in England. The headline says the charge worked. The data says something more interesting."
problem: "England introduced a 5p charge in 2015 and doubled it to 10p in May 2021, extending it to all retailers. The headline result is a drop of about 95% at the large supermarkets in the first year. The question is what the nine years of published data support beyond that headline."
constraints:
  - "Defra's data measures compliance with the charge, not environmental outcome"
  - "Bag-for-life figures are reported voluntarily, so they depend on which retailers chose to report in which year"
  - "Retailers that stop selling single-use bags leave the dataset entirely"
approach: "Rebuilt per-person figures from Defra and ONS population estimates, looked at the 2021 increase as a before-and-after, compared the seven main retailers like for like, and checked what the metadata said about each outlier before interpreting it."
keyDecisions:
  - decision: "Treat the one retailer that barely moved as a definitional problem, not a behavioural one"
    reasoning: "Its figures were repeatedly revised upward to include compostable bags that other retailers leave out of their returns. A naive ranking of supermarkets by plastic would have drawn exactly the wrong conclusion."
    alternatives:
      - "Rank retailers on the reported numbers"
  - decision: "Report what the dataset cannot answer as a finding"
    reasoning: "Whether people switched to bags-for-life is the question that matters environmentally, and reported bag-for-life sales fall from 132 million to zero, which is a reporting artefact, not a collapse in behaviour. Saying the data cannot answer it is more useful than a guess."
techStack:
  - "Python"
  - "pandas"
  - "Statistical analysis"
impact:
  metrics:
    - label: "Bags per person"
      value: "38 in 2016-17 to 7 to 8 by 2020-21, an 81% drop"
    - label: "Around the 10p increase"
      value: "8.6 the year before, then 8.8, 7.7, 7.1 and 7.6, with no visible step down"
    - label: "Main retailers' share of bags"
      value: "63% in 2016-17, 38% in 2024-25, with Tesco, M&S and Waitrose selling none"
  qualitative: "The first 5p did the heavy lifting and the doubling added little. The claim that the charge saved a given tonnage of plastic reaches beyond what the public data supports."
learnings:
  - "When one entity diverges sharply from its peers, the explanation is usually definitional"
  - "A dataset built to monitor compliance cannot be stretched to answer an outcome question"
